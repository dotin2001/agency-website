# Storytelling

## Homepage narrative

### 1. Enter the World

- Narrative objective: introduce the agency positioning and establish a premium, modern, creative first impression.
- Business purpose: make the offer clear quickly and create an immediate path to inquiry or project exploration.
- Intended user emotion: curiosity, confidence, and momentum.
- Required content: main positioning headline, supporting statement, agency name placeholder, primary CTA "Start a Project", and secondary CTA "Explore Our Work".
- Visual direction: large editorial typography, one central transforming 3D object, minimal supporting UI, and a strong cinematic composition.
- Motion direction: Discovery mode with slow reveal, subtle camera movement, and no aggressive or distracting animation.
- Selective 3D role: central object represents abstract creative potential; it enhances memory but must not be required to understand the headline or CTA.
- Primary CTA or interaction: Start a Project, with Explore Our Work as the secondary path.
- Transition into the next chapter: move from curiosity into the client problem.
- Mobile adaptation: prioritize readable headline, visible CTAs, and a simplified or static object treatment.
- Reduced-motion adaptation: show the headline, CTAs, and object in a composed static state with no camera movement.

### 2. The Challenge

- Narrative objective: show understanding of common client and brand problems.
- Business purpose: help qualified visitors recognize their own challenges and see the agency as strategically aware.
- Intended user emotion: recognition, tension, and urgency.
- Required content: concise problem statements about generic brand communication, disconnected campaigns, weak digital experiences, and creative work without measurable purpose.
- Visual direction: fragmented or disconnected visual system with layered typography and content fragments that remain readable.
- Motion direction: Energy mode used as controlled tension; elements may separate, drift, or lose alignment without visual chaos.
- Selective 3D role: central object may fracture or reveal disconnected parts as a spatial metaphor; static HTML copy carries the meaning.
- Primary CTA or interaction: optional scroll prompt into the agency approach; no hard conversion CTA required.
- Transition into the next chapter: fragmented elements begin to reconnect.
- Mobile adaptation: use stacked problem cards or short copy blocks instead of complex overlapping fragments.
- Reduced-motion adaptation: present the fragmented state as a static composition with clear headings and no drifting elements.

### 3. The Transformation

- Narrative objective: introduce the agency approach and capabilities.
- Business purpose: connect client problems to a credible method that balances creative thinking and technical execution.
- Intended user emotion: trust, clarity, and forward motion.
- Required content: capability sequence of Strategy, Creativity, Technology, and Performance, plus value pillars of Strategic clarity, Creative distinction, Digital craftsmanship, and Measurable impact.
- Visual direction: disconnected elements form one connected system; the central object evolves into a structured form.
- Motion direction: controlled Energy mode with scroll-linked assembly and connection.
- Selective 3D role: object transformation may visualize the shift from fragmented potential to structured system, with equivalent text outside the canvas.
- Primary CTA or interaction: continue to selected projects or view related work signals.
- Transition into the next chapter: the connected system becomes the frame for selected work.
- Mobile adaptation: present the sequence as a clear vertical progression with compact capability and pillar labels.
- Reduced-motion adaptation: show the completed system and ordered capability sequence without scroll-linked assembly.

### 4. Selected Projects

- Narrative objective: provide proof through case studies and make projects the strongest homepage section.
- Business purpose: demonstrate capability, relevance, and execution quality before asking for inquiry.
- Intended user emotion: interest, credibility, and desire to inspect details.
- Required content: 3 to 5 selected projects with client or project name, industry, services, short challenge or result statement, project media, and links.
- Visual direction: cinematic, image-led editorial layouts, full-width or layered project presentation, and strong contrast between projects.
- Motion direction: Energy mode with image reveal, perspective depth, smooth zoom or spatial transition, and no overuse of 3D card tilt.
- Selective 3D role: none by default; use only when a project story specifically benefits from spatial treatment.
- Primary CTA or interaction: Explore a Case Study, with View All Projects as the secondary interaction.
- Transition into the next chapter: move from the work to the people behind it.
- Mobile adaptation: use stacked or swipe-friendly project entries with stable media dimensions and visible case-study links.
- Reduced-motion adaptation: replace zoom and spatial transitions with static image states or simple opacity reveals.

### 5. The People

- Narrative objective: humanize the agency and show how collaboration works.
- Business purpose: build trust by showing the people, disciplines, and working culture behind the output.
- Intended user emotion: familiarity, confidence, and approachability.
- Required content: team statement, selected team members or disciplines, working culture, and link to Team page.
- Visual direction: warmer photography, layered portraits, less technological treatment, and more human composition.
- Motion direction: Confidence mode with subtle depth and portrait reveal.
- Selective 3D role: not recommended unless tied directly to agency identity; people and copy should lead.
- Primary CTA or interaction: Meet the Team or link to Team page.
- Transition into the next chapter: move from the team to client perspective.
- Mobile adaptation: keep profiles concise, avoid dense portrait layering, and preserve easy scanning.
- Reduced-motion adaptation: use static portraits and direct content order without depth transitions.

### 6. Client Stories

- Narrative objective: reinforce credibility through client experience.
- Business purpose: add third-party validation and connect work quality to client trust.
- Intended user emotion: reassurance, validation, and readiness.
- Required content: testimonials, client name, role and company, and related project where available.
- Visual direction: large editorial quotes, client marks, and related project backgrounds where approved.
- Motion direction: Confidence mode with slow horizontal or sequential story progression and accessible controls if presented as a slider.
- Selective 3D role: none by default; avoid using 3D for testimonials unless it clarifies a related project story.
- Primary CTA or interaction: browse client stories or move toward inquiry.
- Transition into the next chapter: resolve the story and invite action.
- Mobile adaptation: show one story at a time with keyboard- and touch-accessible controls if interactive.
- Reduced-motion adaptation: disable autoplay and present testimonials as static stacked or stepped content.

### 7. Start the Next Story

- Narrative objective: convert interest into a project inquiry.
- Business purpose: turn accumulated interest, proof, and trust into a clear next step.
- Intended user emotion: readiness, confidence, and ease.
- Required content: final CTA statement, short contact invitation, primary CTA "Start a Project", and optional secondary contact path.
- Visual direction: return of the central hero object in a completed form with a strong but simple composition.
- Motion direction: Confidence mode with visual closure that connects back to the hero.
- Selective 3D role: completed object may return as a lightweight signature, but CTA and contact copy must load independently.
- Primary CTA or interaction: Start a Project, with an optional secondary contact path.
- Transition into the next chapter: no next chapter; route to contact or inquiry flow.
- Mobile adaptation: make the CTA immediate, thumb-accessible, and visually separate from supporting copy.
- Reduced-motion adaptation: show the completed form statically and avoid any looping closure animation.

## Narrative continuity

The homepage should use one central visual metaphor that evolves across the journey:

1. Abstract potential.
2. Fragmentation.
3. Connection.
4. Proof.
5. Human collaboration.
6. Trust.
7. New beginning.

The central 3D object can support this progression, but the page must remain understandable through HTML content, typography, imagery, and CTAs if 3D is disabled, unsupported, simplified for mobile, or reduced for motion sensitivity.

## Planned project case-study journey

### Opening

- Establish the project identity, client context, and immediate result or hook.

### Challenge

- Explain the business, brand, communication, or experience problem.

### Insight

- Show the strategic observation that shaped the creative direction.

### Creative concept

- Present the central idea, narrative, visual system, or experience principle.

### Execution

- Document the deliverables, craft decisions, channels, and production details.

### Impact

- Share measurable outcomes, client response, audience effect, or qualitative value.

### Next story

- Guide visitors to another case study or the contact route.
