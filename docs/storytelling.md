# Storytelling

## Homepage narrative

### 1. Enter the World

- Narrative purpose: introduce the agency atmosphere, standard, and invitation.
- Intended user emotion: curiosity, confidence, and momentum.
- Content requirements: agency name placeholder, positioning line, short value proposition, and primary CTA.
- Visual direction: cinematic first impression with strong composition and restrained detail.
- Motion direction: measured entrance, scroll invitation, and reduced-motion fallback.
- Possible 3D role: optional atmospheric or symbolic scene if it supports the positioning.
- Transition to next chapter: move from agency promise into the client problem.
- Mobile adaptation: keep the message immediate, legible, and CTA-visible without relying on complex animation.

### 2. The Challenge

- Narrative purpose: frame the market or communication problem clients face.
- Intended user emotion: recognition and urgency.
- Content requirements: concise problem statement, common pain points, and stakes.
- Visual direction: contrast, tension, or fragmented layout that remains readable.
- Motion direction: reveal the challenge progressively without hiding core copy.
- Possible 3D role: abstract tension or spatial metaphor only if performant.
- Transition to next chapter: shift from problem to agency transformation approach.
- Mobile adaptation: prioritize short copy blocks and clear hierarchy.

### 3. The Transformation

- Narrative purpose: explain how the agency turns challenge into creative direction and output.
- Intended user emotion: trust and clarity.
- Content requirements: approach, process highlights, service signals, and proof of strategic thinking.
- Visual direction: structured, confident, and less chaotic than the challenge chapter.
- Motion direction: step-based progression tied to content.
- Possible 3D role: simple transformation motif, not a full scene unless justified.
- Transition to next chapter: connect the approach to selected work.
- Mobile adaptation: stack process steps with strong headings and minimal motion.

### 4. Selected Projects

- Narrative purpose: prove capability through work.
- Intended user emotion: interest, credibility, and desire to inspect details.
- Content requirements: featured project titles, categories, short summaries, outcomes, and case-study links.
- Visual direction: image-led project presentation with strong editorial rhythm.
- Motion direction: subtle reveals, project transitions, and hover states where appropriate.
- Possible 3D role: none by default; use only for projects where 3D is part of the story.
- Transition to next chapter: move from work output to the people behind it.
- Mobile adaptation: use swipe-friendly or stacked project cards with clear CTAs.

### 5. The People

- Narrative purpose: humanize the agency and establish working trust.
- Intended user emotion: familiarity and confidence.
- Content requirements: team introduction, roles, working values, and collaboration style.
- Visual direction: warm, direct, and less abstract than earlier sections.
- Motion direction: minimal, respectful reveals and no distracting effects on portraits.
- Possible 3D role: not recommended unless tied to identity.
- Transition to next chapter: connect people to client relationships and outcomes.
- Mobile adaptation: concise profiles and easy scanning.

### 6. Client Stories

- Narrative purpose: show trust, impact, and client perspective.
- Intended user emotion: reassurance and validation.
- Content requirements: testimonial placeholders, client context, measurable or qualitative impact, and attribution.
- Visual direction: editorial quote treatment with supporting project or client context.
- Motion direction: calm pacing, avoid carousel-only access to important content.
- Possible 3D role: none by default.
- Transition to next chapter: turn proof into action.
- Mobile adaptation: show one story at a time with accessible navigation if needed.

### 7. Start the Next Story

- Narrative purpose: convert interest into inquiry.
- Intended user emotion: readiness and ease.
- Content requirements: contact CTA, project-start prompt, contact details placeholder, and expectation setting.
- Visual direction: focused, direct, and high contrast.
- Motion direction: simple entrance and clear interaction states.
- Possible 3D role: not recommended unless extremely lightweight and non-blocking.
- Transition to next chapter: no next chapter; route to contact or inquiry flow.
- Mobile adaptation: make the CTA easy to reach and form path obvious.

## Planned project case-study journey

### Opening

- Establish the project identity, client context, and immediate result or hook.

### Challenge

- Explain the business, brand, communication, or experience problem.

### Insight

- Show the strategic observation that shaped the creative direction.

### Creative concept

- Present the central idea, narrative, visual system, or experience principle.

### Execution

- Document the deliverables, craft decisions, channels, and production details.

### Impact

- Share measurable outcomes, client response, audience effect, or qualitative value.

### Next story

- Guide visitors to another case study or the contact route.
